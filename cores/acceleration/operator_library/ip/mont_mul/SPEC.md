# mont_mul

Montgomery reduction (REDC) engine for the 22-bit modular multiply, one reduction limb per accepted beat. Three products per limb (m=lo*N', m*N_lo and m*N_hi) are formed by the um11 shift-add recode, 11 conditional partial-product adds each, so no Verilog `*` and no hardware multiplier cell appears in this core. The caller supplies the 44-bit product word T=a*b as a[21:0] in the high half and b[21:0] in the low half, and c[2:0] picks one of eight odd moduli whose N'=-N^-1 mod 2^11 is derived in this file by 2-adic Newton iteration. Seed beat (c[31]=1) latches C=T, N, N' and clears the limb counter, and it faults when T>=N*R (R=2^22) because REDC's single conditional subtraction only proves a result below N in the complementary range; for a real product T=a*b with a,b<N that test always passes since N<R. Beats 1 and 2 run one word-based reduction step each: m=(C mod 2^11)*N' mod 2^11, then C <- (C + m*N)/2^11, which is exact because m is chosen to cancel the low limb; beat 3 applies the conditional subtraction C <- C-N when C>=N and sets the converged flag; later beats with c[31]=0 repeat the residue. result=the low 32 bits of the running reduction window, so on the seed beat it is T[31:0] and once converged it is a*b*R^-1 mod N (below N, hence result[31:22]=0). aux[2:0]=modulus index, aux[4:3]=beats of work done (0..3), aux[5]=converged, aux[6]=idle repeat, aux[7]=final conditional subtraction applied, aux[18:8]=the Montgomery multiplier m used by the step just run (zero on a seed or idle beat), aux[31:19]=low 13 bits of the current window. fault=1 on T>=N*R or on a nonzero c[30:3] (malformed modulus selector; c[31] is the seed bit).

See [common interface](../../doc/INTERFACE.md). Latency: 1 enabled edge(s); II=1 when output ready. Independent lanes per accepted beat: 1. Only accepted transactions update state. Reset clears pending output and state, even while CE is zero.

Hardware advantage: A Montgomery step is one 11x11 product to pick the multiplier and two more to fold it into the accumulator; wiring all three shift-add recodes per beat means the modular reduction of a 44-bit product costs 4 enabled beats with no division, no comparison-retry loop and no variable-latency branch, which is the shape a CPU cannot match: its modulo needs a divide (or a reciprocal table plus fix-ups) per reduction and pays it again for every limb.

Independent integer model: `step("mont_mul", a, b, c, state)` in tools/fam_arith.py. Vector rows: 320, of which 128 run on the board. Board-set coverage: 124 distinct responses, 5 fault rows.
