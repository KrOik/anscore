# sha256_round

SHA-256 compression round (FIPS 180-4 section 6.2.2) over the eight working words in state: word j sits at bits 32j..32j+31, j=0 is A up to j=7 is H. The control word is c[5:0]=t plus two load bits. c[31]=1 with c[30]=0 is the restart beat: every word is replaced by the published IV 6a09e667 bb67ae85 3c6ef372 a54ff53a 510e527f 9b05688c 1f83d9ab 5be0cd19 (the first 32 bits of the fractional parts of sqrt(2,3,5,7,11,13,17,19)) before the round is applied, so that beat is round 0 of a new message. c[31]=1 with c[30]=1 is a slot-load beat: it runs no round and only writes a into word c[2:0], which is how the caller installs the previous block hash to chain block 1 and beyond; it answers r=a and x=C0000000 | slot. On a round beat a is the schedule word W t and b is the round constant K t - the 64 K words are the first 32 bits of the fractional parts of cbrt(2..311) and are fed in by the caller, so no 64-entry table sits in the fabric. f=1 with r=x=0 and the window untouched when c[29:6] is nonzero on a round beat, when c[29:3] is nonzero on a slot-load beat, or when c[30] is set without c[31]. The round is T1 = H + Sigma1(E) + Ch(E,F,G) + K + W and T2 = Sigma0(A) + Maj(A,B,C), with Sigma0 = rotr2 ^ rotr13 ^ rotr22, Sigma1 = rotr6 ^ rotr11 ^ rotr25, Ch = (E and F) xor (not E and G) and Maj the majority of A,B,C: XOR, AND, NOT and shift-or rotates only, plus modulo-2^32 adds, never a multiply. The window then moves: H takes G, G takes F, F takes E, E takes D + T1, D takes C, C takes B, B takes A, and A takes T1 + T2. r = new A, x = T1, and the caller adds the eight output words of the final beat back onto the window it installed, which is the add the standard puts at the end of a block.

See [common interface](../../doc/INTERFACE.md). Latency: 1 enabled edge(s); II=1 when output ready. Independent lanes per accepted beat: 1. Only accepted transactions update state. Reset clears pending output and state, even while CE is zero.

Hardware advantage: One SHA-256 round per beat over a 256-bit register state with the Sigma, Ch and Maj cones hardwired: a message block costs 64 beats and no working-set traffic, which is where a scalar core spends most of its cycles.

Independent integer model: `step("sha256_round", a, b, c, state)` in tools/fam_crypto.py. Vector rows: 320, of which 128 run on the board. Board-set coverage: 127 distinct responses, 2 fault rows.
